Each step of the work was checked against a sample or against the previous state of the database, and the checks found mistakes both in the models' work and in my own instructions. The list below records the ones that changed a result, in the order in which they were found. Most were caught in one of three ways: a blind check of a sample, a comparison of the tags before and after a change, or a second look at a number that seemed too good.

- **23 September.** The first hand check of the article tags found that 62% of 50 were right. A running heading copied onto every passage had overridden the chair's announcement of the article under discussion. After four rounds of fixes and checks the figure was 96% (48 of 50).
- **28 September.** A variable in the code that finds the documents a speech cites shadowed another one, so that 289 references pointed at the record in which they were found. A test now covers it.
- **28 September.** Two hand-checked samples were named in a way that made the audit script take them for its own round, and it reported zero without an error. They were renamed and the audit was restored.
- **28–29 September.** Three rules that looked right on the examples were withdrawn after a check. Reading a chair "speaking as the representative of" a state as that delegate removed 24 correct tags. Joining a speaker's line to the fragment before it made 2,948 merges in a dry run, and the narrower version made 93, mostly of separate speakers. Thus, an exemption for points of order was right in only 18 of 27 cases and was narrowed.
- **29 September.** I deleted an uncommitted map of segment identifiers by mistake and rebuilt it from a copy of the earlier database (670 moved segments, 8 split).
- **29–30 September.** The filter that had left out 640 records of the Covenants' meeting series as not about them had read only two pages of each. Reading them in full brought back 90. However, my first correction was too wide: 53 of the 90 were about other items of the Third Committee's agenda, and the threshold had to be replaced by a reading of each record's agenda.
- **30 September.** A change to one row of the concordance made the build refuse 55 correct tags at other meetings. It was found only because the tags were compared with those of the previous build.
- **30 September.** The first version of the ledger above counted 3.9 million tokens generated. The transcripts record a response on several lines, each with the count so far; taking the largest, the figure is 8.3 million.
- **30 September.** The first draft of the model benchmark gave four models nearly the same score on two tasks. The items were at fault, since passages were cut, one task lacked the heading the judges had seen, and the prompt did not say that "rejected by 8 votes to 7" means 7 in favour. The first results were discarded and the items rebuilt.
- **30 October – 1 November.** Speeches whose article had been carried over from the previous debate, where the record has no heading or chair's announcement between speeches on different articles (a chair's passing mention of another article could also switch it), were judged tag by tag by two blind judges (rounds 113 and 122): of 877 such tags, 208 were wrong and refused and 26 moved to the right article. A rule to stop the switch at its source did more harm than good on a blind check (round 114) and was not applied.
- **1 November.** Wider rules for naming the chair (round 124) left the chair's turns of eight opening meetings without a name. A blind check of the round's changes (round 128) found it, and the names were restored.
- **1 November.** A live check of chronological sorting on Article 1 of the UDHR showed `A/C.3/SR.100` sorting before `A/C.3/SR.99`. The masthead of `SR.100` (Tuesday, 12 October 1948) had been misread by the OCR as 10 October 1948; the date was corrected.
- **4 November.** `E/CN.4/SR.98/Corr.1`, one of the 22 documents taken from the University of Virginia's public copy of its travaux, held the text of `A/C.3/SR.289` (19 October 1950), so that six turns of that meeting appeared twice, under a wrong symbol, date and session. The file had been taken from the link on UVA's page for the item, and that page links the wrong file: in UVA's list of the Covenants, only 12 of 469 items link a file named after their own symbol. No step had compared a fetched document's first page with its symbol. The other 21 documents, fetched by file name, were then checked against their first pages and match. UVA's file named after the symbol was fetched, its first page read (the corrigendum of 15 June 1949), and it replaced the wrong one; the fetching script now takes a file by its name.

None of these reached a published version, since nothing has been published yet. Nevertheless, the list will arguably grow with the database.
